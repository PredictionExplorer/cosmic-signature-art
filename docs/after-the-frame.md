# After the frame — the true ending, and the object behind the picture

_A plan for the venue's next phase, written 9–10 November 2026 (UTC) on the owner's ask: a work of art by a world-class artist, visitors who "fall out of their chair" and are moved, some to tears, grounded in the literature, with a codebase to match. It answers to the [north star](north-star.md) and follows [First Light](first-light.md)._

## 0. In one minute

The venue astonishes in concept and proves its physics. It does not yet move anyone, and a newcomer is never told what they are looking at.

The research is plain about why. Astonishment and being moved are different responses with different causes. Awe comes from vastness. Being moved comes from closeness visibly changing: a bond, a separation, a return, a loss, watched and not caused. Beauty and scale alone do not do it.

Every work here already holds that story, and the venue hides it. Three bodies stay together for the million steps a work records. Then the film ends, and the venue stops looking. What happens next is in the record, proved to the last bit, and offered as a small switch below the fold.

So the plan is three things, in this order:

1. **Say what is true.** An audit of all 72 works found that most "eternal dances" end, that five recorded "ejections" are the arithmetic failing, and that six pictures hold such a failure inside their own window. The venue says so, in its record, before it stages anything.
2. **Give every work its ending.** One true sentence under every still. Then, after the frame, with the camera held where the film left it: a body leaves the picture, and comes back, or does not. The visitor chooses one of the three to stay with. It ends on the unaltered still.
3. **Show the object behind the picture.** Each Light still is one face of a real sculpture of light in depth. Turned in the hand, it stays as rich as the still.

Sound, the museum's essay and the codebase follow. Tears cannot be promised by any site; the plan aims at being moved, and measures it.

## 1. What we found

### 1.1 On the site (a desk at 1440 × 900, 9 November)

1. **The live 3D drawing is a thin wire; the still is silk.** First Light and the turned orbit draw three thin glowing lines. The first review called the turned orbit "anemic".
2. **A newcomer is never told what they are looking at.** The plate's label reads "Orbit Ribbons · Harmonic Weave", the generator's vocabulary. On a first visit the only word on screen for ten seconds is "Skip".
3. **A work has no ending.** The show trails off into a row of word-controls below the fold, then the record.
4. **The venue is silent**, and the owner has never decided on sound.
5. **A moved visitor has nothing to do**: no share control, and no way to ask to be told of the next birth.

### 1.2 In the generator (its source, read 9 November)

- Every Light still is drawn from one primitive: a soft straight stroke, added as light. Slow passages are drawn wide and dim, fast ones fine and bright.
- The strokes are strung in three dimensions: along each body's path, from each body to its own place a little later, and between the bodies. The generator then turns the event to a chosen view and flattens it.
- It scores four seeded views of each event and keeps one. The venue's frame registry holds all four scores.
- So for 55 of the 72 works the still is one face of a real object in depth. The other 17 need their own words: 11 draw phase space, and 9 are kaleidoscopes whose copies are made after flattening (3 are both).
- An offline sketch confirmed it on № 031 and № 023: the same strokes, turned, stay rich where today's turned view is a wire.

## 2. What the evidence says

Four fact-checked briefs were written for this plan (aesthetic emotion; sound; interactive works known to move people; the generator's renderer). Each marked what it could and could not verify. The claims the plan rests on:

**Astonishment and being moved are different responses.**

- **Awe** comes from vastness that makes a viewer revise their understanding (Keltner and Haidt 2003). Its famous after-effects, on generosity, time and well-being, mostly failed preregistered replication (Graziosi et al. 2026), so the venue never promises what awe "does".
- **Being moved** comes from closeness visibly changing: separation, reunion, loss, witnessed and not caused (Menninghaus et al. 2015; Schubert et al. 2018; Zickfeld et al. 2019, in 19 nations). Sadness feeds it (Hanich et al. 2014).
- **Beauty and scale alone do not move people.** With abstract paintings in a museum, visitors were moved only moderately and none cried; content and personal relevance predicted it, not colour, light or scale (Zhou and Leder 2026).

**Three points of light are enough to carry a story.** 33 of 34 viewers described three moving shapes as beings (Heider and Simmel 1944). The effect survives the loss of shape but not of continuous motion (Scholl and Tremoulet 2000). The venue need invent nothing; its words state facts.

**What moves a person is personal, and a stake helps.** Viewers barely agree on which artworks move them (r = .13; Vessel, Starr and Rubin 2012). In games, moving moments involve loss, attachment and a lack of agency (Bopp, Mekler and Opwis 2016).

**Shape in time matters.** Chills arrive after seconds of build-up and cluster at closures (Wassiliwizky et al. 2017). Peak and end predict how an experience is remembered (r = .58 over 174 effects; Alaybek et al. 2022), though the end's special weight is disputed (Tully and Meyvis 2016).

**One honest sentence, then silence; teach afterwards.** Information raises understanding reliably and liking only sometimes: three recent studies found no gain (Krauss et al. 2021; Szubielska and Imbir 2021; Castellotti et al. 2024). Making sense of a pleasant surprise shortens the pleasure (Wilson et al. 2005). Awe makes people notice what they do not know (McPhetres 2019).

**Sound helps, and can harm.** Music that fits raises the emotion felt at pictures (Baumgartner et al. 2006), but background music lowered liking for artworks (Loureiro et al. 2019) and disliked music lowered their beauty (Janzen et al. 2022). Sonifications draw warm self-reports of wonder and learning, most from blind and low-vision listeners (Arcand et al. 2024); no controlled study of emotion exists. The brief's judgement: a sound made only from the motion should bring wonder and some chills, not tears.

**What ruins it on the web.** A slow or busy first frame: appeal is judged in about 50 ms (Lindgaard et al. 2006). Moving the view for the visitor: 35 % of US adults over 40 showed vestibular dysfunction on a balance test (Agrawal et al. 2009), and WCAG 2.3.3 asks that such motion can be turned off. Sound nobody asked for (WCAG 1.4.2). Company at the encounter (Pelowski et al. 2014).

**How likely are tears? Unlikely, and no site can promise them.**

- No study has measured tears at a website. Cameras found tears in fewer than one in ten viewings of films chosen to move people (Zickfeld et al. 2020). Three of 25 testers cried at the game Journey, by its maker's count.
- The brief's estimate for a first visit to a visual-art website: nearer one visitor in a hundred than one in ten.
- The odds rise with a story of bond and loss, a personal link, sound, solitude, a large screen, minutes rather than seconds, and a return visit.
- So the plan aims at being moved, at awe, and at a lump in the throat, and measures them. It does not make crying the test.

## 3. What a panel of designers changed

A first draft proposed a sculpture, a sound and an ending in which the camera drew back as a body left. Nine designers were asked to take it apart, each blind to the others: an installation artist, a composer, a game designer, a museum developer, a technical artist, a curator, a psychologist, a first-time visitor and collector, and a speaker for Ink. All nine wrote; the three judges who were to score them did not finish, for want of usage.

They agreed to a striking degree. Seven said to hold the frame, or to put the chosen body's frame first. Seven found, each with the venue's own code, that the fates had never been audited. Seven said the order was backwards. Six asked for one body to stay with, and five for an ending on the unaltered still.

## 5. Decisions for the owner

1. **"Eternal dance."** The published trait stays as published. In its own voice the venue already says "bound through 8,000,000 steps". Recommended: keep to that everywhere, and never say "eternal" itself.
2. **The works whose computation fails.** Recommended, and built in §8.1: the works stay exactly as they are; the record says what was found; nothing past a failed pass is called an escape.
3. **Looking past the record** (step 4). It is the one moment whose outcome the published record does not hold. It also shows collectors that most dances end. Recommended: yes, after the offline record is committed.
4. **Sound** (step 6). A proof behind a flag, heard before anything ships. Recommended.
5. **The generator.** Its makers could be told what the audit found, so that later cycles use a step that follows close passes.

## 6. Rules held throughout

- The art is never altered; derived views are labelled and never on sale surfaces.
- Motion comes from the art, and so does sound. The view moves only by the visitor's act.
- Light and Ink are equal: every moment above has its form on paper.
- Reduced-motion parity, 44 px targets, the 12 px floor, eight languages, keyboard and screen-reader paths.
- The work is the first paint.
- The words state facts and name no feelings. Nothing is asked at the peak or in the rest after it, and never near a sale.

## 7. How we will know

- **People, first.** Five who did not build it, each alone on their own phone. One tap on the Affect Grid before and after; ten items from AESTHEMOS (being moved, awe, fascination, boredom, confusion); the bodily signs from KAMMUS Two; "In one sentence, what did you watch?"; "I would show this to someone." Run on today's site as the baseline, then after steps 2 and 3 with the arms the panel disagreed on: the fate told or withheld; the hand live or standing aside; silence or sound. A week later: what do they remember, unprompted?
- **The record.** Every sentence generated from a record is tested over all 72 works. Every number the venue states is re-derived by a committed script.
- **The gates**, as for every change: lint, types, format, fonts, unit tests with coverage, script tests, a production build, Playwright on six local projects and Firefox on CI.
- **A second independent review**, scored as the first was.

## 8. As built

### 8.1 The energy the computation keeps

_10 November 2026, on the branch `claude/after-the-frame`: step 0._

**What a visitor sees.**

- **In the record** (act two of a work page, and Verify an orbit), beneath the record's note, one more sentence says what the published record does not: whether the computation kept its energy.
  - № 031: "As computed here, the energy holds to 12 digits through the recorded window."
  - № 004: the same to 10 digits, then "Near step 1,535,000 two bodies pass 0.11 apart, closer than the computation's fixed step can follow, and its energy changes by 780%. Gravity keeps energy constant, so past that step this is the computation, not an orbit."
  - № 022: only the second sentence, for step 44,000: the pass lies inside the recorded window.
- **After the frame**, from the step of such a break on, the line under the drawing reads "Not an orbit past step 1,535,000: the computed energy changed by 780%." It no longer says a body "has escaped".
- The record's own row is untouched: the published fate is still met to the step, and still marked exact.

**How it is built.**

- `EnergyWatch` (`src/lib/physics/orbit.ts`) rides the fate run the worker already makes. Every thousand steps it asks what energy the bodies carry and how far that stands from the window's first value; every step it notes how close any two bodies came. A change of more than one part in a thousand is dated once, with the closest pass since it last asked.
- `DerivedFate.energy` carries the answer to the page. Nothing is compared with the record, because the generator publishes no such measure.
- `energyText` and `shareText` (`src/components/orbit-proof.ts`) word it once for both pages, in eight languages.
- `beyondLine` (`src/components/orbit-beyond.ts`) says it past the break.

**What was checked.** Unit tests for the watch, on synthetic bodies and on № 054 (held), № 004 (broken past its window) and № 022 (broken inside it); the words in English and Japanese; the section and the verifier. In the browser, on a production build, in Chromium and WebKit on desks, phones and with reduced motion.

**What it does not do yet.** The word _To the ejection_ still names the record's event on the five works where that event is the arithmetic failing; step 3 recomposes those controls. _(Gone since §8.3.)_

### 8.2 One true sentence

_10 November 2026, on the branch `claude/one-true-sentence`: step 2._

**What a visitor sees.**

- **Under every still on a work page**, where the label line stood, one line of two sentences at most, from that work's record:
  - № 025: "Three bodies, together for all 1,000,000 steps this work records. 6.3 seconds after its film ends, one of them is no longer bound to the other two."
  - № 031: the same first sentence, then "They are still bound to one another at step 8,000,000, where the record stops looking."
  - № 004, whose computation loses its energy before its dated ejection: the first sentence alone.
- **On the home**: "Three bodies, each pulling on the other two." Then "This is the ink their motion stirred." under the Ink still, or "This is a long exposure of their motion." under the Light.
- **On a phone** the strip's centre used to give way to the words, so nothing was said at all. The line is now the first thing under each picture, the twin's too.
- The generator's vocabulary ("Orbit Ribbons · Harmonic Weave") has left the strip. It keeps its place in the entry below, where it has its notes. A sale's page keeps its label: the viewer cuts the line there, as it cuts the orbit.

**How it is built.**

- **A record of each fate's energy.** `npm run art:energy` (`scripts/build-orbit-energy.mjs`) runs each of the 72 works from its stored window to its published fate with the `EnergyWatch` of §8.1, the fate run the browser's worker also makes. It writes `src/lib/art/orbit-energy.json` (27 KB, read on the server) only when every run meets the published fate to the body and the step. 57 runs keep their energy; 15 lose it. An entry binds to its work only while the seed and the trait file's hash agree; the file is read only while it was measured against today's tolerance and each entry agrees with itself. The script test that re-derives every orbit from its seed holds all 72 entries to that derivation.
- **The line.** `fateLine` and `homeLine` (`src/lib/art/fate-line.ts`) are pure: a record and that measure in, the sentences out. A work page words its line on the server; the home's sentences are in the catalog. Eight languages. Japanese and Chinese run two sentences together without a space (`t.sentences`), and the record's energy sentence of §8.1 now does too.
- **The strip** (`src/components/plate.tsx`, `artwork-viewer.tsx` and their styles). Each sentence is a box of its own, so a line breaks between two sentences before it breaks inside one. The widest single sentence in any of the eight languages is 594 px at the strip's 15 px italic; the line's measure is 660 px.
  - **1280 px and wider:** inside the 56 px strip, beside the numeral, a sentence to a line. The strip's three parts are raised 3 px together, so the second line stands clear of the film's score. The centre stays wider than the widest sentence while a film's readout, and then _Again_ and _After the frame_, join the words; the least room is Vietnamese at 1280 px, 649 px for a sentence of 588.
  - **768 to 1279 px:** a row of its own beneath the numeral and the words, because the centre cannot hold a sentence beside the words there once a film's readout joins them: at 900 px the line wrapped to three and the strip grew 9 px while the film played. The plate gives up the row's 60 px, so the work, its words and its line stand in one screen. At 1024 × 768 the still is 934 px wide where it was 1,024, and its `sizes` hint says so, so a tablet is not sent a wider file than its plate.
  - **That wide, and no taller than 560 px** (a phone on its side): the picture keeps all its room and the strip its one row. The line is not shown there; it is still read aloud.

### 8.3 After the frame, with the frame held

_10 November 2026, on the branch `claude/held-frame`: the first slice of step 3._

**What a visitor sees.**

- **The frame is held.** Past the recorded window the camera stays where the film left it. A body that goes far crosses the edge of the picture, and nothing follows it.
- **While it runs** the published still stands back to 30 %, so three bodies and their trails can be read over it, and the picture carries its label under it: "Analysis, not the work". One small line says how far past the film the orbit is, and who is out of the picture:
  - "00:04 past the film · body 2 is out of the picture, 0.1 picture widths away"
  - "00:08 past the film · bodies 2 and 3 are out of the picture"
  - "00:10 past the film · all three are out of the picture"
- **It ends where the picture resolves**, not at a step the record dates (the table below).
- **Then the still, alone.** The drawing gives way to it over 1.2 seconds. For eight seconds nothing is said and nothing is offered. Then one line of what happened, in the book face, and two words: _Again_, and _The record_, which goes to the proof.
  - № 025: "Body 2 left the picture 3.4 seconds after the film ended. 7.2 seconds later the other two had left it too. The record finds body 2 no longer bound to the other two at step 1,210,000."
  - № 031: "Body 3 left the picture 33.6 seconds after the film ended. It came back 8.8 seconds later. All three are still bound to one another at step 8,000,000, where the record stops looking."
  - № 014, whose body 2 had left once and come back: "Body 2 last left the picture 28.7 seconds after the film ended. The other two had already left it. The record finds …"
  - № 016, whose pair stays: "Body 3 left the picture 42.2 seconds after the film ended. 8.0 seconds later the other two were still in it. The record finds …"
  - № 004, whose computation loses its energy: "Past step 1,535,000 this is not an orbit: the computed energy changed by 780%." No body is called free, and the three are not called bound.
- **The wait is the visitor's to cut.** _Further on_ goes to four seconds before the next moment (a leaving, a return, all three out). It keeps its place among the words for the whole performance, dimmed while it would save less than four seconds more. _Tell me_ says what happened at once, with no rest.
- **Where motion is unwelcome** (reduced motion, the venue paused, the hush) nothing plays and nothing fades. The picture stands at the film's end, _Further on_ steps it to a second past each moment and then to the end, and from the end to the same line. No step is timed.
- **The whole picture in one screen.** The orbit section is taller than a screen: with the picture's top under the running head, its line and its words were below the fold. Past the frame the caption's account of the window and the window's score step aside; the label, the line and the words stand under the picture; and the page brings the picture 6 px under the head as the performance begins. Nothing moves again until the visitor leaves: the label, the line and the words keep their room through the performance, the rest and the telling. Back at the frame the word that was pressed is put back under the hand.
- **On the plate only.** A work drawn in its own plane, for want of a frame that binds, has no frame for a body to leave, and is not offered After the frame. Today all 72 are drawn on their plates.

**Where a performance ends.** A body is in the picture while its centre is on it. An absence _lasts_ when it runs three seconds of film or to the path's end. A visitor _sees the body go_ when a lasting absence also takes it a tenth of the picture's width away.

- **The line and the fact count differently, on purpose.** The line names every body in an absence that lasts, however near the edge it stands: a body past the edge by more than its own light is out of sight. In № 051 all three are out for 41 seconds, and the line says so. The moments of a performance and its ending are made only of absences a visitor sees the body go on: a body that stands just past the edge for a while has not gone anywhere.
- **"Left", "left again", "last left".** A body's first leaving after the film is "left". A later one it came back from is "left again" (9 works, № 002 and № 041 among them). A later one the path ends inside is "last left" (15 works, № 014 and № 047 among them).
- **The record's sentence follows the picture's.** "The record finds body 2 no longer bound to the other two at step 1,210,000" is said only where the record dates that ejection and the path reaches the step that confirms it. "All three are still bound to one another at step 8,000,000" only where the record dates none and the energy held to the horizon. Where the path stops at lost energy, the last sentence says that (16 works).
- **Eleven works name no body.** A phase portrait, a hodograph or a cross-braid plot does not draw the bodies where they were, and a cross-braid mark is made of two bodies' coordinates: in № 019 the mark called "body 3" left the picture because body 1's _y_ went far. There the marks' leaving still shapes the performance, the line counts only the time, and what is told is the record's finding alone (№ 054: "The record finds body 2 no longer bound to the other two at step 1,090,000.").
- **Light and Ink.** The frame is one for both materials, but Light draws every stroke again about a work's symmetry and Ink the first copy alone, and each is told of its own picture. So № 008 (drawn twelve times) is told "It came back 51.0 seconds later" on Light and "52.6" on Ink; the other 71 are told the same. Turn the plate while the line stands, and it is told again.
- **A path cut by lost energy does not end on an instant.** № 030's energy breaks seven seconds past the step that confirms its escape, 0.17 seconds after the last of the three crossed the edge. That is not an emptied picture: it is told as "Body 3 last left the picture 1.9 minutes after the film ended. The record finds body 3 no longer bound to the other two at step 4,830,000. Past step 5,106,000 this is not an orbit: the computed energy changed by 0.3%."
- Past a confirmed ejection the path runs on for a minute of film. In every work but № 016 and № 030 all three are out of the picture by then, the slowest 49 seconds after the confirmation.
- A performance runs a median of 47 seconds. 31 run past a minute and 19 past two, which is why _Further on_ and _Tell me_ are always in reach. Three (№ 022, 066, 068) have nothing to perform, because the energy broke inside the recorded window: they say their line at once, and offer no _Again_.
- These counts are of each work's Light plate. The venue states none of them; a script test holds this section to them (below).

**Why the picture's event, and not the record's.** §8.2 found that the record's date is the step at which the generator's test first finds a body free, not when it goes: in 13 of 27 works the body is still within the window's span at that step, and in others it left seconds before. After the frame used to stop there and say "Body 2 has escaped". It now ends on what the visitor sees, a body crossing the edge of the picture, and says the record's finding after it, as the record's. The record's own row in the proof says the same now: "body 2 no longer bound at step 1,210,000", where it said "escapes".

**How it is built.**

- **The path** (`src/lib/physics/beyond.ts`). The worker that derives the orbit carries it on past the window once, from the bodies the fate run kept, with the generator's own integrator and the energy watch of §8.1. It places every hundredth step on the work's plate with the camera held (`HeldPlacement`). It stops at the record's horizon, or a minute of film past a confirmed ejection, and never past a step at which the energy broke: the samples since the watch last asked are dropped. It is the worker's last reply, after the window in depth, so the orbit in the hand does not wait for it. A bound orbit's path is 1.7 MB; № 008's, with twelve copies, 20 MB.
- **What the picture shows** (`src/lib/physics/absences.ts`). Pure functions over the path: each body's times out of the picture and how far it went, where a performance ends, and the moments worth going straight to. They know nothing of canvases.
- **The performance** (`src/components/orbit-beyond.ts`). The page plays the path back; nothing is integrated on the main thread. `beyondLine` and `beyondFact` word it, each sentence only where it is true.
- **The section** (`src/components/orbit-score.tsx` and its styles). Three phases: running, resting, told. The reading of the path is made once for a path and a material, since it is the same at every size (38 ms for № 008 in Node on the machine this was built on, 2 ms at the median). At the rest the last frame is left standing while the stylesheet fades the drawing's layers and brings the still back, on the venue's own `--t-sit`. The words are one keyed list, so a word that stays from one state to the next is the same element, and the hand or the keyboard that pressed it still stands on it. The page is brought to the picture two frames after the layout changes, to one absolute place: WebKit cancels a smooth scroll begun in the frame of a change it has to answer.
- **Room.** A desk's stage is now as tall as the screen less 220 px (it was 200), which leaves 166 px beneath the picture past the frame: the label, three lines of what happened, and the words, with 8.5 to spare. A phone keeps five lines and two rows of words, the narrowest six. A phone on its side has its plate fitted to the height left, so the whole picture is in view, small.
- **Keyboard and screen reader.** When the words go for the rest, a focus that stood on one stays in the section. The line is live once the performance is over, so what happened is said when it comes; asked for by _Tell me_, it takes the focus. While it runs the line changes four times a second and is not announced.
- **Words.** Seventeen keys in eight languages. _To the ejection_ and "has escaped" are gone. In Korean a body is now "2번 천체" where a particle follows, so none hangs on a numeral.
