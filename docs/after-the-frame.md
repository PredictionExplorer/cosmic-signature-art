# After the frame — the true ending, and the object behind the picture

_A plan for the venue's next phase, written 9–10 November 2026 (UTC) on the owner's ask: a work of art by a world-class artist, visitors who "fall out of their chair" and are moved, some to tears, grounded in the literature, with a codebase to match. It answers to the [north star](north-star.md) and follows [First Light](first-light.md)._

## 0. In one minute

The venue astonishes in concept and proves its physics. It does not yet move anyone, and a newcomer is never told what they are looking at.

The research is plain about why. Astonishment and being moved are different responses with different causes. Awe comes from vastness. Being moved comes from closeness visibly changing: a bond, a separation, a return, a loss, watched and not caused. Beauty and scale alone do not do it.

Every work here already holds that story, and the venue hides it. Three bodies stay together for the million steps a work records. Then the film ends, and the venue stops looking. What happens next is in the record, proved to the last bit, and offered as a small switch below the fold.

So the plan is three things, in this order:

1. **Say what is true.** An audit of all 72 works found that most "eternal dances" end, that five recorded "ejections" are the arithmetic failing, and that six pictures hold such a failure inside their own window. The venue says so, in its record, before it stages anything.
2. **Give every work its ending.** One true sentence under every still. Then, after the frame, with the camera held where the film left it: a body leaves the picture, and comes back, or does not. The visitor chooses one of the three to stay with. It ends on the unaltered still.
3. **Show the object behind the picture.** Each Light still is one face of a real sculpture of light in depth. Turned in the hand, it stays as rich as the still.

Sound, the museum's essay and the codebase follow. Tears cannot be promised by any site; the plan aims at being moved, and measures it.

## 1. What we found

### 1.1 On the site (a desk at 1440 × 900, 9 November)

1. **The live 3D drawing is a thin wire; the still is silk.** First Light and the turned orbit draw three thin glowing lines. The first review called the turned orbit "anemic".
2. **A newcomer is never told what they are looking at.** The plate's label reads "Orbit Ribbons · Harmonic Weave", the generator's vocabulary. On a first visit the only word on screen for ten seconds is "Skip".
3. **A work has no ending.** The show trails off into a row of word-controls below the fold, then the record.
4. **The venue is silent**, and the owner has never decided on sound.
5. **A moved visitor has nothing to do**: no share control, and no way to ask to be told of the next birth.

### 1.2 In the generator (its source, read 9 November)

- Every Light still is drawn from one primitive: a soft straight stroke, added as light. Slow passages are drawn wide and dim, fast ones fine and bright.
- The strokes are strung in three dimensions: along each body's path, from each body to its own place a little later, and between the bodies. The generator then turns the event to a chosen view and flattens it.
- It scores four seeded views of each event and keeps one. The venue's frame registry holds all four scores.
- So for 55 of the 72 works the still is one face of a real object in depth. The other 17 need their own words: 11 draw phase space, and 9 are kaleidoscopes whose copies are made after flattening (3 are both).
- An offline sketch confirmed it on № 031 and № 023: the same strokes, turned, stay rich where today's turned view is a wire.

### 1.3 In the orbits (audited 10 November)

Every work was carried from the first step of its recorded window to step 60,000,000 with the venue's own integrator and fate test, measuring the computed energy every thousand steps. All 36 published ejections were re-derived to the same body and step.

## 2. What the evidence says

Four fact-checked briefs were written for this plan (aesthetic emotion; sound; interactive works known to move people; the generator's renderer). Each marked what it could and could not verify. The claims the plan rests on:

**Astonishment and being moved are different responses.**

- **Awe** comes from vastness that makes a viewer revise their understanding (Keltner and Haidt 2003). Its famous after-effects, on generosity, time and well-being, mostly failed preregistered replication (Graziosi et al. 2026), so the venue never promises what awe "does".
- **Being moved** comes from closeness visibly changing: separation, reunion, loss, witnessed and not caused (Menninghaus et al. 2015; Schubert et al. 2018; Zickfeld et al. 2019, in 19 nations). Sadness feeds it (Hanich et al. 2014).
- **Beauty and scale alone do not move people.** With abstract paintings in a museum, visitors were moved only moderately and none cried; content and personal relevance predicted it, not colour, light or scale (Zhou and Leder 2026).

**Three points of light are enough to carry a story.** 33 of 34 viewers described three moving shapes as beings (Heider and Simmel 1944). The effect survives the loss of shape but not of continuous motion (Scholl and Tremoulet 2000). The venue need invent nothing; its words state facts.

**What moves a person is personal, and a stake helps.** Viewers barely agree on which artworks move them (r = .13; Vessel, Starr and Rubin 2012). In games, moving moments involve loss, attachment and a lack of agency (Bopp, Mekler and Opwis 2016).

**Shape in time matters.** Chills arrive after seconds of build-up and cluster at closures (Wassiliwizky et al. 2017). Peak and end predict how an experience is remembered (r = .58 over 174 effects; Alaybek et al. 2022), though the end's special weight is disputed (Tully and Meyvis 2016).

**One honest sentence, then silence; teach afterwards.** Information raises understanding reliably and liking only sometimes: three recent studies found no gain (Krauss et al. 2021; Szubielska and Imbir 2021; Castellotti et al. 2024). Making sense of a pleasant surprise shortens the pleasure (Wilson et al. 2005). Awe makes people notice what they do not know (McPhetres 2019).

**Sound helps, and can harm.** Music that fits raises the emotion felt at pictures (Baumgartner et al. 2006), but background music lowered liking for artworks (Loureiro et al. 2019) and disliked music lowered their beauty (Janzen et al. 2022). Sonifications draw warm self-reports of wonder and learning, most from blind and low-vision listeners (Arcand et al. 2024); no controlled study of emotion exists. The brief's judgement: a sound made only from the motion should bring wonder and some chills, not tears.

**What ruins it on the web.** A slow or busy first frame: appeal is judged in about 50 ms (Lindgaard et al. 2006). Moving the view for the visitor: 35 % of US adults over 40 showed vestibular dysfunction on a balance test (Agrawal et al. 2009), and WCAG 2.3.3 asks that such motion can be turned off. Sound nobody asked for (WCAG 1.4.2). Company at the encounter (Pelowski et al. 2014).

**How likely are tears? Unlikely, and no site can promise them.**

- No study has measured tears at a website. Cameras found tears in fewer than one in ten viewings of films chosen to move people (Zickfeld et al. 2020). Three of 25 testers cried at the game Journey, by its maker's count.
- The brief's estimate for a first visit to a visual-art website: nearer one visitor in a hundred than one in ten.
- The odds rise with a story of bond and loss, a personal link, sound, solitude, a large screen, minutes rather than seconds, and a return visit.
- So the plan aims at being moved, at awe, and at a lump in the throat, and measures them. It does not make crying the test.

## 3. What a panel of designers changed

A first draft proposed a sculpture, a sound and an ending in which the camera drew back as a body left. Nine designers were asked to take it apart, each blind to the others: an installation artist, a composer, a game designer, a museum developer, a technical artist, a curator, a psychologist, a first-time visitor and collector, and a speaker for Ink. All nine wrote; the three judges who were to score them did not finish, for want of usage.

They agreed to a striking degree. Seven said to hold the frame, or to put the chosen body's frame first. Seven found, each with the venue's own code, that the fates had never been audited. Seven said the order was backwards. Six asked for one body to stay with, and five for an ending on the unaltered still.

## 5. Decisions for the owner

1. **"Eternal dance."** The published trait stays as published. In its own voice the venue already says "bound through 8,000,000 steps". Recommended: keep to that everywhere, and never say "eternal" itself.
2. **The works whose computation fails.** Recommended, and built in §8.1: the works stay exactly as they are; the record says what was found; nothing past a failed pass is called an escape.
3. **Looking past the record** (step 4). It is the one moment whose outcome the published record does not hold. It also shows collectors that most dances end. Recommended: yes, after the offline record is committed.
4. **Sound** (step 6). A proof behind a flag, heard before anything ships. Recommended.
5. **The generator.** Its makers could be told what the audit found, so that later cycles use a step that follows close passes.

## 6. Rules held throughout

- The art is never altered; derived views are labelled and never on sale surfaces.
- Motion comes from the art, and so does sound. The view moves only by the visitor's act.
- Light and Ink are equal: every moment above has its form on paper.
- Reduced-motion parity, 44 px targets, the 12 px floor, eight languages, keyboard and screen-reader paths.
- The work is the first paint.
- The words state facts and name no feelings. Nothing is asked at the peak or in the rest after it, and never near a sale.

## 7. How we will know

- **People, first.** Five who did not build it, each alone on their own phone. One tap on the Affect Grid before and after; ten items from AESTHEMOS (being moved, awe, fascination, boredom, confusion); the bodily signs from KAMMUS Two; "In one sentence, what did you watch?"; "I would show this to someone." Run on today's site as the baseline, then after steps 2 and 3 with the arms the panel disagreed on: the fate told or withheld; the hand live or standing aside; silence or sound. A week later: what do they remember, unprompted?
- **The record.** Every sentence generated from a record is tested over all 72 works. Every number the venue states is re-derived by a committed script.
- **The gates**, as for every change: lint, types, format, fonts, unit tests with coverage, script tests, a production build, Playwright on six local projects and Firefox on CI.
- **A second independent review**, scored as the first was.

## 8. As built

### 8.1 The energy the computation keeps

_10 November 2026, on the branch `claude/after-the-frame`: step 0._

**What a visitor sees.**

- **In the record** (act two of a work page, and Verify an orbit), beneath the record's note, one more sentence says what the published record does not: whether the computation kept its energy.
  - № 031: "As computed here, the energy holds to 12 digits through the recorded window."
  - № 004: the same to 10 digits, then "Near step 1,535,000 two bodies pass 0.11 apart, closer than the computation's fixed step can follow, and its energy changes by 780%. Gravity keeps energy constant, so past that step this is the computation, not an orbit."
  - № 022: only the second sentence, for step 44,000: the pass lies inside the recorded window.
- **After the frame**, from the step of such a break on, the line under the drawing reads "Not an orbit past step 1,535,000: the computed energy changed by 780%." It no longer says a body "has escaped".
- The record's own row is untouched: the published fate is still met to the step, and still marked exact.

**How it is built.**

- `EnergyWatch` (`src/lib/physics/orbit.ts`) rides the fate run the worker already makes. Every thousand steps it asks what energy the bodies carry and how far that stands from the window's first value; every step it notes how close any two bodies came. A change of more than one part in a thousand is dated once, with the closest pass since it last asked.
- `DerivedFate.energy` carries the answer to the page. Nothing is compared with the record, because the generator publishes no such measure.
- `energyText` and `shareText` (`src/components/orbit-proof.ts`) word it once for both pages, in eight languages.
- `beyondLine` (`src/components/orbit-beyond.ts`) says it past the break.

**What was checked.** Unit tests for the watch, on synthetic bodies and on № 054 (held), № 004 (broken past its window) and № 022 (broken inside it); the words in English and Japanese; the section and the verifier. In the browser, on a production build, in Chromium and WebKit on desks, phones and with reduced motion.

**What it does not do yet.** The word _To the ejection_ still names the record's event on the five works where that event is the arithmetic failing; step 3 recomposes those controls.
