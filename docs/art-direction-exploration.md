**Cosmic Signature — a museum of transformations**

Creative exploration, October 22, 2026. This responds to the user's stronger brief: an exceptionally artistic, surprising and beautiful digital venue whose first purpose is to astonish. The earlier quiet-museum direction remains a useful discipline, but it is no longer the full creative ambition. The user subsequently selected The Passage and The Atlas for visual exploration and authorized code only for mockups. The [three-page mockup set](../concepts/passage-atlas-v1/README.md) now demonstrates those directions with untouched actual artwork. Detailed layouts remain subject to review. All approved auction rules remain unchanged; no production frontend has been implemented.

Latest steering: movement should organize the whole experience, and Ink should be primary. The [v2 moving mockup](../concepts/passage-atlas-v2/README.md) now opens with the actual Ink film, uses a material curtain to enter Light, and animates a three-work Atlas around the visitor's selection. The rhythm has two layers: intricate fluid motion within the work, slow deliberate travel of whole media surfaces around it. The following earlier storyboard remains conceptual background; v2 reverses its original Light-first entrance. This changes presentation, not token identity, media provenance or auction rules.

**The artistic proposition already exists in the work.**

One recorded identity produces a choreography that can appear as luminous color, ink carried through fluid, a finished image, and a film. Future interpretations may give it volume or another material presence. The site's signature experience should be the moment a visitor discovers another expression of the same work.

The color and ink deserve equal artistic status. “Light” and “Ink” are useful working labels for interpretations; neither should be called a filter, a cosmetic skin, or the lesser version. The work retains its token identity, history and sale state as its presentation changes. These labels are proposed public copy, not shipped catalog strings.

I inspected real paired reference images for tokens 23, 31 and 35, plus generator documentation and source. Their empty black fields and pale paper are part of the composition. The presentation should preserve those authored intervals. The ink is an actual fluid-based rendering, not a desaturated color image. The technical evidence and external references are collected in [Art presentation research](art-presentation-research.md).

**Four directions offer different kinds of astonishment.**

| Direction | Defining experience | Strength | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Passage | The same work carries the visitor from emitted light into ink on paper | An identity specific to Cosmic Signature; immediately communicates multiple interpretations | Becoming a repeated theme-switch gimmick |
| The Atlas | A composed field of art rearranges around a chosen visual relationship | Makes the entire growing collection worth exploring | Too many tiny works or disorienting motion |
| The Unfolding Book | An artist's publication with monumental plates, diptychs and films inside its pages | Cultural seriousness, strong typography and endless editorial variation | Looking static or requiring hand-layout for every work |
| The Screening Room | A work unfolds in time and resolves into its final still | Gives the films the attention their medium deserves | Imposed pacing, heavy downloads and a compulsory intro |

The recommended synthesis is The Passage for the defining encounter, The Atlas for discovery, and The Unfolding Book for editorial composition. The Screening Room becomes a deliberate mode within individual works. This should feel like one authored experience, not four competing navigation systems.

**The Passage: a concrete first encounter.**

Imagine arriving at an immense luminous Signature suspended in black. The image is immediately present. The wordmark is precise and unobtrusive. One identity line and two or three quiet choices occupy the edges. A pale strip hints at another material beyond the dark field.

“See in ink” initiates the defining transition. A clean, continuous field of paper moves through the composition, revealing the genuine ink rendition in the same viewing position. The title and token identity remain anchored. The interface's light changes with the material: near-white type in the luminous view, charcoal type in the paper view. It feels like the work has changed the room around it.

This is stagecraft between real assets, not a claim that color pixels physically transform into fluid ink. Do not invent intermediate NFT images or distort the authored output to force a match. If a restrained dissolve proves more convincing than a travelling reveal, use it. The intention is recognition followed by surprise.

The visitor can choose Film and watch the work unfold. At its end, the film settles into its corresponding still and rests. The caption becomes more present only once attention has had time to land. A single word, “Again,” can offer a replay. The work should be allowed to finish.

Nothing requires waiting through the introduction. Collection, exhibitions and the work's direct URL are available immediately. A shortened transition gives reduced-motion users the same conceptual change. The theatrical reveal belongs to deliberate changes of material, not every navigation click.

**The Atlas: make the collection an experience of relationships.**

The first collection view is a composed field of a few substantial works, with one large anchor and several quieter companions. Their scales and spacing feel like an exhibition hang or a major art-book spread. It should remain composed as the viewport changes rather than becoming a limitless cloud of thumbnail cards.

Selecting a work can open it directly or invite a relationship: Follow this color, Follow this form, See its cycle. The selected work stays visually anchored while nearby works change. The motion helps the eye understand what remained and what changed. Curated relationships are explicitly editorial; computed relationships must come from verified measurements rather than invented affinity scores.

Switching the collection's interpretation reveals the same token identities in ink. It changes the visual reading of the archive. Use prepared thumbnails for that state rather than loading every full-size film. If an interpretation is unavailable for a work, say so and preserve its place rather than inventing a substitute.

A conventional all-works grid and search remain a direct route. They are the index at the back of the exhibition, not the emotional opening. Shareable selection/filter state and a reliable return to the previous location keep the expressive exploration from becoming a maze.

Possible editorial trails include The Long Curve, Almost Still, Turbulence, and Light Becoming Trace. These are proposed curatorial titles, not new token names or claims about mathematical classification. They can bring old works into new conversations without privileged auction-only curation.

**The Unfolding Book: give the project an authored voice.**

Use the structure of an exceptional artist's publication: a strong cover, an expansive plate, a surprising change of scale, a diptych, a small note, then another expansive plate. A huge title may introduce an exhibition, while the following screen gives almost all its space to one work. Blank space has compositional weight.

Develop a distinctive typographic identity rather than importing the app's dashboard hierarchy. An incisive display face or carefully drawn wordmark can carry the identity; a highly legible companion carries captions and longer reading. Technical notation belongs to the deeper record. Font choices, licensing and multilingual companions need design exploration; a fashionable serif alone is not a complete direction.

The portfolio dimension also needs a human artistic position. Develop a short statement with the artist about why one choreography is expressed through different materials and what a visitor should notice. It should sound authored, not like a protocol explainer. A possible working editorial line is “An orbit, remembered in light and ink.” This is proposed copy, not an attributed artist quote. Let the longer physics explanation follow that artistic invitation.

The color/ink diptych is a natural recurring spread. Both full compositions are visible at equal scale and carry one identity. At narrow widths they become successive plates or a deliberate material change, not two unreadable miniatures. Native artwork proportions determine the layout.

Large film plates can live inside this publication. A Film action activates only that plate, while everything around it remains still. Future spatial work can occupy a plate with its own orbit/reset controls. The publication metaphor does not require simulated paper curls, fake binding or a virtual building.

**The Screening Room: take time seriously as a medium.**

The color film reveals an accumulating trajectory; the ink film records fluid evolution. The normal versions can correspond in source time for compatible renderer versions. A visitor should be able to watch Light, change to Ink, and remain at the corresponding moment when that mapping is verified.

A paired viewing mode could place the two films side by side with one transport. It should feel like watching one choreography expressed in two materials. Use actual source-frame correspondence. The slower ink films have their own timing metadata and may trim a blank pre-roll; matching timestamps or multiplying by a nominal speed factor is not sufficient.

The long ink studies deserve a dedicated slow viewing option. They contain additional simulated frames and can reveal fine eddies more calmly. There is no need to load them before the visitor chooses that experience. An unobtrusive elapsed-time line is enough; full player controls remain available.

The films are finite, not seamless ambient loops. A hold on the final still followed by an intentional replay respects that structure. Reverse playback may be an inspection tool, but must not imply a physically reversed fluid process. A scrubbed frame is also not automatically the canonical finished artwork.

Sound is a possible future commission, not assumed existing media. If introduced, derive or compose it deliberately, identify its authorship, and make it opt-in. Silence should remain a complete experience.

**The artwork page is the heart of the museum.**

An individual page should have three degrees of attention:

1. Encounter: one enormous work with its identity and discreet viewing controls.
2. Study: alternate interpretations, still/film, paired viewing, selected details, and a short explanation of the process.
3. Record: provenance, seed, renderer and media versions, ownership/custody, license, files, and auction history.

The first screen should not require reading a description to understand that the art matters. When a visitor asks for detail, the information should be exact and beautifully composed. Essential controls must still be legible; tiny inaccessible labels do not make a site more prestigious.

Ideas for the study layer include a museum-like detail plate with a clearly identified crop, a reversible move from full composition to a chosen detail, and a concise explanation showing how three moving bodies produce both kinds of mark. Keep the untouched full composition immediately recoverable. Any cursor-responsive ambient surface should remain outside the artwork itself.

**One work, an expandable family of interpretations.**

Do not hard-code a permanent two-sided Light/Ink switch. Treat the work's stable identity separately from its available interpretations and presentations:

| Level | Current examples | Future room |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work | One NFT, seed and provenance record | Same identity persists as presentations expand |
| Interpretation | Light; Ink | Another authored interpretation such as a spatial fluid work |
| Presentation | Finished still; normal film; available long film | Interactive volume, installation view, printable study |
| Study lens | Spectral separation within the Light interpretation | Other truthful, clearly described analytical or artistic views |

The interface can begin with two elegant interpretation choices and expand into a small shelf when a third is genuinely available. Each interpretation exposes only the presentations it supports. A 3D film is not an interactive 3D object; an interactive object should have actual camera controls and a reliable reset. Do not show unavailable modes as promises about what every buyer will receive.

Future interpretations should supplement preserved historical media, not silently overwrite the record of a previous sale. A visitor's presentation preference does not modify the NFT or turn each view into a separate collectible. Renderer authenticity and official-package policy remain open technical specification work in the decision register.

**Spectral exploration could be a particularly distinctive side chamber.**

The pipeline already separates the completed luminous image into wavelength bands and produces a sweep film. A quiet “Through the spectrum” lens could let the visitor watch some structures brighten while others disappear. This is grounded in existing output, but is different from watching the orbit unfold in time.

Present the effect visually first, then offer a short explanation. Place the control within a study surface, with a single clear wavelength indication if needed. Do not turn the opening experience into a laboratory instrument panel. Availability must come from each work's actual media package, not the mere existence of a generator capability.

**Existing work and new exhibitions deserve the same artistic care.**

An eight-work cycle exhibition can have a designed title spread, a slow introduction, and individual plates. Its opening presentation can juxtapose works by visual rhythm while a conventional list provides every lot and its exact state. The exhibition remains worth visiting after all sales finish.

The permanent collection should also gain editorial exhibitions across cycles. Curation changes how work is encountered, never which assigned tokens exist or whether the approved eight are offered. Existing collectors should see their works included as part of the same artistic body, not relegated to an inferior archive.

A daily featured work or an optional programmed screening can create a reason to return. Select it consistently for a visit; do not swap the work under someone while they inspect or bid. Treat any timed artistic intervention as opt-in away from transaction review.

**Collecting is a deliberate second layer.**

Let a visitor fall into the work before seeing its financial record. A quiet availability indication and a clear Auctions route preserve discoverability. “Collect this work” opens a stable side panel or mobile sheet with the actual token, bid amount, minimum next bid, deadline and refund consequences. The artwork can stay visible, but its theatrical transitions pause while a person reviews or signs a transaction.

Every approved auction rule remains in force. No artistic countdown may replace the contractual clock. Changing interpretation must not switch lots, alter bid state, or obscure that the buyer is collecting the same token. A sold work keeps its full museum page. No wallet is required to experience the art.

**A small set of moments should receive disproportionate craft.**

- The arrival: a complete work is visible immediately, before heavy media arrives.
- The passage: Light to Ink changes the material atmosphere while preserving identity.
- The reveal: a film resolves into its native final still and rests.
- The affinity: the collection reorganizes around one chosen visual relationship.
- The expansion: a chosen work becomes a full viewing experience without losing context.

These moments need careful timing, typography and asset preparation. The ordinary controls can remain exceptionally simple. Constant motion would flatten the difference between an important encounter and navigation.

On mobile, give the visitor one generous work at a time, clear interpretation controls, and vertical editorial rhythm. On desktop, allow more spatial and paired composition. Reduced-motion, low-bandwidth and static viewing should preserve the art and the conceptual relationships. Performance is part of the aesthetic: a stalled reveal breaks the experience.

**What I would explore first, before implementation.**

Use real paired works 23, 31 and 35 to produce: a desktop Light/Ink encounter, its narrow-screen equivalent, one exhibition spread, and an atlas composition. Include one exceptional Atlas rearrangement in the first study: the chosen work holds its place while a new constellation of related works forms around it. Then test a short motion storyboard of the passage and a film-to-still ending using authentic assets. Compare whether the experience remains compelling after the first reveal, and whether future interpretation choices fit without redesigning the page.

Do not commit to a full 3D website merely to signal technical ambition. The current 3D lab could later support a genuine special exhibition once its outputs and provenance are suitable. The first version already has an unusually strong artistic relationship to reveal.

The proposed first release concentrates on the Passage, the permanent artwork page, one excellent identity-preserving Atlas interaction, and eight-work exhibition pages. A broad affinity engine, programmed screenings and the spectral study can expand later. The first version should already have discovery worth returning to, so its distinctiveness does not depend on seeing a reveal effect once. These are design priorities, not implementation authorization.
